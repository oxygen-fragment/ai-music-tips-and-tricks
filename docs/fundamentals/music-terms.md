# Music Terms in Plain English

No theory knowledge required. Just clear explanations of what you're hearing.

---

## Sound Elements

### Melody

The tune you hum. The "notes" part of the song.

**Think of it like:** The main character in a movie. Everything else supports it.

**Examples:**

- The "Happy Birthday" tune
- The part you sing in the shower
- What the lead guitar or singer usually does

**In songs:** Usually one main melody, sometimes answered by secondary melody.

### Harmony

Supporting notes that make the melody richer. Multiple notes played together.

**Think of it as:** Background singers or chord progressions that fill out the sound.

**Examples:**

- Choir singing different notes simultaneously
- Guitar chords under a vocal melody
- Bass note + melody note = harmony

**Simple test:** If you can hum it easily, it's probably melody. If it's the "other notes" making it sound full, that's harmony.

### Rhythm

When notes happen. The timing pattern.

**Think of it as:** The difference between speech (free-flowing) and a drumbeat (structured timing).

**Examples:**

- Clapping along to a song = following rhythm
- Drum patterns
- When syllables hit in lyrics

**Not the same as:** Tempo (which is speed). Rhythm is the pattern, tempo is how fast you play the pattern.

### Tempo

How fast or slow the song moves. Measured in BPM (beats per minute).

**Think of it as:** The song's speedometer.

**Examples:**

- 60 BPM = one beat per second (like a clock ticking)
- 120 BPM = twice as fast
- Your resting heartbeat ≈ 60-80 BPM

**See:** [Beats & Rhythms](beats-and-rhythms.md) for full BPM breakdown.

### Timbre (pronounced TAM-ber)

The "color" or "texture" of a sound. Why different instruments playing the same note sound different.

**Think of it as:** The difference between your voice and your friend's voice saying the same word.

**Examples:**

- Piano vs guitar playing middle C = different timbre
- Whispered vocals vs belted vocals = different timbre
- Bright sound vs warm sound = timbre descriptions

**Descriptive words for timbre:**

- Warm, cold, bright, dark
- Smooth, rough, harsh, soft
- Rich, thin, full, hollow

## Musical Techniques

### Cadence

How a musical phrase ends. Musical punctuation.

**Think of it as:**

- Period (.) = strong cadence (feels finished)
- Comma (,) = weak cadence (feels like more is coming)

**Why it matters:** Creates expectation and resolution. Keeps listeners engaged.

**In AI prompts:** Usually automatic, but you can request:

- "Unresolved ending" (weak cadence) see [Unresolved Ending Cadence Examples](/samples/cadence/unresolved-ending.md)
- "Definitive ending" (strong cadence) see [Definitive Ending Cadence Examples](/samples/cadence/definitive-ending.md)

### Dynamics

Loud vs soft. Volume changes throughout the song.

**Think of it as:** The emotional intensity dial.

**Common terms:**

- Forte (f) = loud
- Piano (p) = soft (yes, confusing with the instrument)
- Crescendo = gradually getting louder
- Diminuendo = gradually getting quieter

**In songs:** Creates drama and prevents monotony.

## Sound Effects/Textures

### Reverb

**What it is:** Spaciousness. Echo/reflection that makes sound seem like it's in a physical space.

**Real-world equivalent:**

- Small room = short reverb
- Cathedral = long, dramatic reverb
- Bathroom = bright, obvious reverb

**Why use it:** Adds depth, makes vocals sit in the mix, creates atmosphere.

**In AI prompts:**

- "Cathedral reverb"
- "Intimate, dry vocals" (no reverb)
- "Spacey atmosphere"

---

### Distortion

**What it is:** Intentional audio "fuzziness" or "grittiness."

**Think of it as:** Taking a clean sound and roughing it up.

**Examples:**

- Electric guitar overdrive
- Screaming/growling vocals
- Lo-fi/crackly aesthetic

**Why use it:** Adds aggression, energy, or vintage character.

**In AI prompts:**

- "Distorted guitar"
- "Clean tone" (opposite)
- "Gritty vocals"

---

### Staccato

**What it is:** Short, detached, choppy notes.

**Real-world equivalent:**

- Speaking in. Short. Bursts. (staccato)
- vs speaking smoothly in connected phrases (legato)

**Why use it:** Creates tension, urgency, or playfulness.

**In AI prompts:**

- "Staccato strings"
- "Choppy rhythm"
- "Short, punchy notes"

---

### Legato

**What it is:** Smooth, connected, flowing notes. Opposite of staccato.

**Real-world equivalent:** Singing a long phrase in one breath without gaps.

**Why use it:** Creates smoothness, elegance, emotional connection.

**In AI prompts:**

- "Smooth, flowing melody"
- "Legato strings"
- "Connected vocal line"

---

### Syncopation

**What it is:** Emphasizing the off-beats instead of main beats.

**Think of it as:** Musical surprise. Notes hitting where you don't expect them.

**Examples:**

- Reggae's choppy guitar (hits between drum beats)
- Funk bass lines
- Makes you want to move in unexpected ways

**Why use it:** Creates groove, interest, prevents predictability.

**In AI prompts:**

- "Syncopated rhythm"
- "Off-beat emphasis"
- "Funky groove"

---

### Crescendo / Diminuendo

**What it is:**

- Crescendo: Gradually getting louder
- Diminuendo (or Decrescendo): Gradually getting quieter

**Think of it as:** Volume fades, but done intentionally for effect.

**Why use it:** Build tension (crescendo) or wind down (diminuendo).

**In AI prompts:**

- "Building intensity"
- "Gradual fade out"
- "Explosive build-up"

## Genre Descriptors by Feeling

### Ballad

**Feels like:** Slow, emotional, story-driven
**Tempo:** 60-80 BPM
**Characteristics:** Emphasis on lyrics, often romantic or sad

### Uptempo

**Feels like:** Fast, energetic, makes you move
**Tempo:** 120-140+ BPM
**Characteristics:** Dance-friendly, high energy

### Ambient

**Feels like:** Atmospheric, background-worthy, minimal vocals
**Tempo:** Variable, often slow
**Characteristics:** Texture-focused, mood over melody

### Lo-fi

**Feels like:** Relaxed, slightly fuzzy, study music vibes
**Tempo:** 70-90 BPM usually
**Characteristics:** Imperfect quality (intentional), nostalgic

## Quick Reference: Describing What You Hear

**If the sound is...**

| You hear... | The term is probably... |
|-------------|------------------------|
| The part you hum | Melody |
| The background notes | Harmony |
| How fast it is | Tempo (BPM) |
| When notes hit | Rhythm |
| Spacey/echoey | Reverb |
| Fuzzy/aggressive | Distortion |
| Choppy/detached | Staccato |
| Smooth/flowing | Legato |
| Surprising timing | Syncopation |
| Getting louder | Crescendo |
| Getting quieter | Diminuendo |
| The unique "voice" of the sound | Timbre |

## Using These Terms in AI Prompts

**Don't overthink it.** AI tools understand both:

- Technical: "Staccato strings, crescendo into chorus, 120 BPM"
- Descriptive: "Choppy violins that build into an explosive hook, upbeat"

**Both work.** Use whatever feels natural to you.

**Experiment:**

- Same prompt with "staccato" vs "legato"
- Same prompt at 80 BPM vs 140 BPM
- Same lyrics with "warm acoustic" vs "bright synth"

Notice how terms change the vibe dramatically.
